Our favorite New York

A few of our favorite spots to eat, drink, explore, and enjoy while you're in town. Welcome to our city!

Eat

Food & coffee

Some of our go-to spots and longtime favorites across the city.

Levain Bakery

Upper West Side  ·  Bakery

If you only have room for one indulgence, make it this. Levain's legendary cookies are thick as a paperback and warm from the oven. The original on W 74th St started it all, with locations around the city now.

Levain Bakery →

Katz's Delicatessen

Lower East Side  ·  Jewish deli

A New York institution since 1888. If you've seen When Harry Met Sally, you know the scene. Order the pastrami on rye, grab a number, sit in the glorious chaos of the dining room, and take it all in.

Katz's →

Russ & Daughters

Lower East Side  ·  Appetizing

Four generations of the finest smoked salmon, herring, and bagels in the city — open since 1914. A pilgrimage for any serious eater. Also check out their cafe on Orchard St for a full sit-down spread.

Russ & Daughters →

See & Do

Things to explore

Museums, parks, and experiences worth making time for — many of them free.

Shakespeare in the Park

Central Park  ·  Free theater

Free outdoor Shakespeare at the Delacorte Theater every summer — one of New York's great traditions. Use the virtual lottery or line up early; tickets go fast but the experience is genuinely unforgettable.

Public Theater →

MoMA PS1

Long Island City, Queens  ·  Contemporary art

One of the great contemporary art museums — and free admission this year. Strong rotating exhibitions, a great permanent collection, and an iconic courtyard. Easy to pair with a Williamsburg visit.

MoMA PS1 →

Tenement Museum

Lower East Side  ·  History

Guided tours through preserved tenement apartments telling the real stories of immigrant families who lived here from the 1860s onward. Deeply moving and impeccably researched. Book ahead — tours sell out.

Tenement Museum →

Museum of Reclaimed Urban Space

East Village  ·  Urban activism

A tiny, free gem documenting NYC's history of squatters, community gardens, and grassroots urban activism. Passionate guides, fascinating stories, and a window into a side of New York most visitors never find.

MoRUS →

The NYC Store

City Hall area  ·  Books & souvenirs

The city's official store — a hidden gem for urban planning books, maps, and only-in-New-York gifts. The best souvenirs in the city that nobody seems to know about. Near City Hall Park.

NYC Store →

Gallery Hopping in Chelsea

Chelsea  ·  Art galleries

Dozens of world-class contemporary art galleries packed into a few blocks around W 24th–27th St. All free to enter, no appointment needed. Pick up art cards from the galleries as affordable, beautiful souvenirs.

Elizabeth Street Garden

Nolita  ·  Garden

A beloved, sculpture-filled community garden tucked between buildings in Nolita. A quiet, beautiful oasis in the middle of everything. One of those New York spots that feels like a secret even when it's busy.

Staten Island Ferry

Lower Manhattan  ·  Free

The best free thing in New York. A 25-minute ride with spectacular views of the Statue of Liberty and the Manhattan skyline. Runs 24/7, no ticket needed. Get on, get off, get right back on the return.

Circle Line Cruise

Midtown  ·  Boat tour

A classic 2.5-hour cruise around the entire island of Manhattan. Touristy in exactly the right way — great commentary, great views, and a completely different perspective on the city you thought you already knew.

Circle Line →

Neighborhoods

Our corners of the city

The two neighborhoods we'd always point people toward first.

West Village

Manhattan  ·  Nick's pick

Cobblestone streets, beautiful brownstones, and incredible restaurants tucked into every corner. An unhurried pace that feels rare for Manhattan. Start on Bleecker St and let yourself get lost — that's the whole point.

Lower East Side

Manhattan  ·  Daniel's pick

Rich with immigrant history, home to Katz's and Russ & Daughters, and full of a creative energy unlike anywhere else in the city. Start at the Tenement Museum, walk down Orchard St, and end at a bar that looks like a bodega.
