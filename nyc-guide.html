Our favorite New York

A few of our favorite spots to eat, drink, explore, and enjoy while you're in town. Welcome to our city!

Williamsburg

Near the venue

The wedding is in the heart of Williamsburg — one of Brooklyn's most walkable and vibrant neighborhoods. Here are a few spots worth exploring while you're in the area.

Smorgasburg

Williamsburg  ·  Outdoor food market

New York's iconic open-air food market, held every Saturday at Marsha P. Johnson State Park right on the East River waterfront. Around 100 local vendors, incredible views of the Manhattan skyline, and some of the most creative food in the city. Perfect for the morning after the wedding.

Smorgasburg →

Levain Bakery — Williamsburg

Williamsburg  ·  Bakery

Levain's legendary thick-as-a-paperback cookies, right in the neighborhood. The Williamsburg outpost is just a short walk from the Pod hotel — an easy stop the morning after the wedding. Get there early; they sell out.

Levain Bakery →

Domino Park

Williamsburg  ·  Waterfront park

A beautiful waterfront park built on the site of the old Domino Sugar refinery, with sweeping views of the Manhattan skyline. The park preserves some of the original industrial machinery as public sculpture.

City Reliquary

Williamsburg  ·  Community museum

A tiny, wonderful community museum dedicated to the everyday artifacts and history of New York City — old subway tokens, neighborhood ephemera, vintage postcards, and objects donated by New Yorkers over the decades. The kind of place that could only exist in this city, and that most New Yorkers have never been to.

City Reliquary →

Buffalo Exchange & 2nd Street Vintage

Williamsburg  ·  Vintage & thrift

Two great spots for secondhand shopping within easy walking distance of the venue. Buffalo Exchange (Bedford Ave) is a well-curated buy-sell-trade shop with a reliable turnover of quality pieces. 2nd Street Vintage is a smaller, more eclectic find — the kind of place where you stumble on something you didn't know you needed.

Buffalo Exchange →

Daniel's picks

The Lower East Side

Daniel's favorite neighborhood in New York City. Rich with immigrant history and full of a creative energy unlike anywhere else in the city. Start at the Tenement Museum, walk down Orchard St, and end at a bar that looks like a bodega.

Katz's Delicatessen

Lower East Side  ·  Jewish deli

A New York institution since 1888. If you've seen When Harry Met Sally, you know the scene. Order the pastrami on rye, grab a number, sit in the glorious chaos of the dining room, and take it all in.

Katz's →

Russ & Daughters

Lower East Side  ·  Appetizing

Four generations of the finest smoked salmon, herring, and bagels in the city — open since 1914. A pilgrimage for any serious eater. Also check out their cafe on Orchard St for a full sit-down spread.

Russ & Daughters →

Tenement Museum

Lower East Side  ·  History

Guided tours through preserved tenement apartments telling the real stories of immigrant families who lived here from the 1860s onward. Deeply moving and impeccably researched. Book ahead — tours sell out.

Tenement Museum →

Dorothy Strelsin Memorial Community Garden

Lower East Side  ·  Garden

A little piece of quiet and greenery in the middle of the city. Daniel used to live in the building right across the street during an internship in New York.

Museum of Reclaimed Urban Space

East Village  ·  Urban activism

A tiny, free gem documenting NYC's history of squatters, community gardens, and grassroots urban activism — right on the border of the LES and East Village. Passionate guides, fascinating stories, and a window into a side of New York most visitors never find.

MoRUS →

Theater & Live Events

Shows & experiences

New York does live performance better than anywhere. A few highlights worth planning around.

New York Neo-Futurists

East Village  ·  Experimental theater

One of New York's most genuinely original theater companies — best known for Too Much Light Makes the Baby Go Blind, a show of 30 plays in 60 minutes performed in a rotating, audience-driven order every weekend. No two shows are ever the same. A downtown institution and a perfect introduction to what makes New York theater special.

NY Neo-Futurists →

Oh, Mary!

Broadway  ·  Comedy

Cole Escola's wildly funny Broadway debut — a historical comedy romp starring Escola as a bored, chaotic Mary Todd Lincoln itching to be a cabaret star. One of the buzziest and most talked-about shows on Broadway right now. Bring your sense of the absurd.

Oh, Mary! →

Cats: The Jellicle Ball

Broadway  ·  Musical

A bold reimagining of Cats set in Harlem's ballroom scene, with an almost entirely Black and queer cast. Exhilarating choreography, stunning voguing, and a complete reinvention of source material you thought you knew. One of the most exciting things to happen to Broadway in years.

Cats: The Jellicle Ball →

Death Becomes Her

Broadway  ·  Musical

The 1992 dark comedy film gets the full Broadway musical treatment — camp, spectacle, and some of the most inventive staging you'll see. A love letter to excess and a genuinely great night out.

Death Becomes Her →

On the water

See the city from the water

Some of the best views of New York are from the water — and several of the city's most iconic rides are free or close to it.

Staten Island Ferry

Lower Manhattan  ·  Free

The best free thing in New York. A 25-minute ride with spectacular views of the Statue of Liberty and the Manhattan skyline. Runs 24/7, no ticket needed. Get on, get off, get right back on the return.

NYC Ferry

Citywide  ·  Ferry network

A surprisingly wonderful way to get around the city — and one that stops right in Williamsburg, making it an easy connection to and from the wedding. Routes run up and down the East River with stops in Manhattan, Brooklyn, and Queens. A scenic alternative to the subway that not enough visitors know about.

NYC Ferry →

Circle Line Cruise

Midtown  ·  Boat tour

A classic 2.5-hour cruise around the entire island of Manhattan. Touristy in exactly the right way — great commentary, great views, and a completely different perspective on the city you thought you already knew.

Circle Line →

Museums

Art & culture

From world-class contemporary art to grassroots urban history — New York's cultural institutions are endlessly worth your time.

MoMA PS1

Long Island City, Queens  ·  Contemporary art

One of the great contemporary art museums — and free admission this year. Strong rotating exhibitions, a great permanent collection, and an iconic courtyard. Easy to pair with a Williamsburg visit.

MoMA PS1 →

The Whitney Biennial

Meatpacking District  ·  Contemporary art

The Whitney Biennial is one of the most important surveys of contemporary American art — and it's running this year. Held at the Whitney Museum of American Art in the Meatpacking District, with stunning Hudson River views. Always provocative, always worth the conversation it starts.

Whitney Museum →

The New Museum

Bowery  ·  Contemporary art

One of the most consistently exciting contemporary art museums in the city — known for bold, boundary-pushing exhibitions and a striking stacked building on the Bowery. Right on the edge of the Lower East Side, making it a natural pairing with a day in the neighborhood.

New Museum →

Gallery Hopping in Chelsea

Chelsea  ·  Art galleries

Dozens of world-class contemporary art galleries packed into a few blocks around W 24th–27th St, especially between 10th and 11th Avenue. All free to enter, no appointment needed. Pick up art cards from the galleries as affordable, beautiful souvenirs.

The NYC Store

City Hall area  ·  Books & souvenirs

The city's official store — a hidden gem for urban planning books, maps, and only-in-New-York gifts. The best souvenirs in the city that nobody seems to know about. Near City Hall Park. Daniel's ritual every time he comes to New York is to pick up a new urban planning book here.

NYC Store →

For families

Bringing kids?

New York is a fantastic city for children — a few of our favorite family-friendly picks.

Daniel Tiger at the Bronx Zoo

The Bronx  ·  Family

The Bronx Zoo is one of the largest urban zoos in the world and a great day out for kids of any age — and this summer it's hosting a Daniel Tiger exhibit. A perfect outing for little ones who need a break from city exploring.

Bronx Zoo →

NYC Transit Museum

Brooklyn Heights  ·  Museum

Housed in a decommissioned 1936 subway station in Brooklyn Heights, this museum is a genuine delight — especially for kids. Vintage subway cars you can walk through, original turnstiles, signal equipment, and the full story of how the city's transit system was built. One of the best family-friendly options in the city.

NYC Transit Museum →
